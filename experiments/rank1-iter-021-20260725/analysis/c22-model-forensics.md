## Strongest simulator sensitivity

The only large, model-consistent sensitivity is:

```text
default-visible supporting meaning present
vs
supporting meaning hidden/collapsed
```

C10 fell from C7 `30/101` to `15/101`. No other treatment moved the
authoritative outcome by a comparable amount. The simulator's normal path
also scans nearly the full page before clicking Basic.

Directionally, C4 and C7 suggest the evaluator values:

- recognizable situations and concrete outcomes;
- a believable learning sequence;
- default-visible evidence that practice is structured.

The C20 frozen proxies add only exploratory support:

- the programme-dominant subgroup attempted at `4/9 = 44.4%`;
- the injury-dominant subgroup attempted at `2/19 = 10.5%`;
- non-attempted paths spent longer in the injury section;
- nearly every usable path still reached and clicked Basic.

These are partial, post-hoc associations. They do not prove that changing
attention causes conversion.

### What is not a demonstrated sensitivity

- Basic reach or clickability;
- scroll speed or total duration;
- number of visible actions;
- programmatic focus;
- form layout, validation or persistence;
- default selection;
- ARIA semantics;
- plan-card alignment;
- calendar or progress widgets;
- image primacy;
- repeated price, logistics, data-state or plan-fit copy.

Once a normal path focuses the first field, completion remains effectively
deterministic. C22 must not optimize downstream form mechanics.

No candidate below has an evidence-supported route to `41/101`. Ranges are
honest planning bands for one 101-request cohort, not confidence intervals.

### 1. `structured-program-evidence-salience`

```text
classification      = causally untested, legal, weak
predictedOrders     = 27–34 / 101
mostLikely          = 30–31 / 101
hardPassProbability = low, approximately 2–5%
```

Treatment boundary:

- start from exact C7;
- preserve all programme words and all six `.program-module` nodes in their
  current order;
- change only visual treatment inside the programme section so the modules
  read as one coherent sequence from assessment to final scenario;
- allowed examples: consistent visible numbering and stronger module-header
  hierarchy;
- no hiding, disclosure, section move, image, CTA, form or JavaScript change.

Activation evidence:

1. C10 proves the evaluator is sensitive to visible supporting content.
2. C20's programme-dominant proxy subgroup is directionally stronger than its
   injury-dominant subgroup.
3. The treatment is encountered before the stable Basic-click decision by
   almost every normal simulator path.

Disqualifier:

- if implementation requires changing programme copy, hiding details,
  reordering sections or adding an action, it overlaps a closed family and
  must not ship.
